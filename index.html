How pay-per-use billing works

₹ 4.20 / kWh
  1. 1. Solar electricity supplied to the farm is measured in kWh by the energy meter.
  2. 2. Metered units are billed at the agreed rate — ₹4.20 per kWh in this demo.
  3. 3. Usage is settled monthly; the dashboard statement shows generation, consumption and any export.
  4. 4. Any grid export credit depends on DISCOM approvals, net-metering rules and the signed agreement.
Supplying electricity to third parties may require specific approvals. The final model must be checked against local electricity-supply rules before it is offered commercially.

Student venture concept · Version 1.0

Solar-powered electricity for sustainable farming.

Eco Farm Tech proposes a solar system with a monitoring dashboard that helps farm operators see how much clean electricity they generate, where it is used and what it costs — so irrigation and electrical loads can be planned around the sun.

Demo prototype Not connected to live hardware Andhra Pradesh, India Clean energy · Smart farming

The problem

Farm operations depend on a reliable electricity supply for water pumping and irrigation. Power interruptions and energy expenses can affect farm productivity, and many farms have no clear view of how much energy each activity actually consumes.

Our proposed solution

Photovoltaic panels generate electricity from sunlight, an inverter converts it for farm loads, and an optional battery stores surplus. A monitoring dashboard shows generation, consumption and system status so the operator can act on it.

How the system works

Solar → inverter → meter → farm loads
1. Sunlight

Available solar resource, which changes with weather, season and time of day.

2. Solar panels

PV modules convert sunlight into direct-current (DC) electricity.

3. Protection

DC protection, combiner boxes, isolators, surge protection and earthing.

4. Inverter

Converts DC into alternating current for pumps and farm equipment.

5. Meter & distribution

Measures energy supplied or consumed and feeds the farm distribution board.

6. Farm loads

Irrigation pump, lighting and other equipment — with an optional battery for later use.

The dashboard is the last link in the chain: it displays monitoring data so the operator can understand system performance. It does not measure or control electricity by itself.

Who it is for

  • Farmers using electric irrigation pumps
  • Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs)
  • Agricultural cooperatives
  • Farmhouses and rural businesses
  • Small agro-processing units

Benefits

  • Uses renewable solar energy
  • Can reduce dependence on grid electricity
  • Visibility into generation and consumption
  • Expandable with demand and available space

Risks & considerations

  • Output varies with weather, season and shading
  • Batteries add cost and need safe maintenance
  • Equipment may need repair or replacement
  • Grid export needs approvals and agreements
